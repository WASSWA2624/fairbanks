## B. Background and approach (35 points)

### B1. Problem (Uganda / East Africa last mile)

Uganda faces repeated infectious disease threats shaped by geography, rapid urban growth, and high population movement. Outbreaks that start in peri-urban communities can grow before national systems see them. Americans and Ugandans are safer when threats are found and contained close to source.

In FairBanks catchments, CHWs and VHTs already visit homes and schools, and the Medical Centre sees patients every day. Too often, community reports, clinic records, and lab referrals still sit apart. The 2023 JEE praised progress and still named gaps in information sharing, subnational surge capacity, and use of data for early action — including stronger integrated surveillance such as NISS.

FairBanks proposes to close the last-mile gap under MoH leadership: structured community and facility signals, faster notify-respond loops with districts, trained frontline workers, and tools that feed government systems rather than create a parallel silo.

### B2. Approach and model

CDC and partner countries need outbreaks stopped closer to where people live. FairBanks already runs a clinic, CHW/VHT outreach, and working FCHIP tools — ready to feed MoH/NISS pathways under government leadership.

FairBanks Community Reach cascade stays the operating model: community members -> CHWs/VHTs -> Community Reach programmes -> Medical Centre -> research and skills -> economic empowerment. FCHIP is the intelligence component on the Data & Feedback loop.

Award funds will support surveillance systems, training, emergency readiness, data linkages, and public health programme coordination. Routine clinical care continues on FairBanks' own operations and is not the purpose of this cooperative agreement.

We will collaborate with MoH, district/KCCA teams, other CDC partners, and community structures. Software, SOPs, and training materials developed under the award will be available to MoH and CDC for appropriate use.

### B3. Year 1 strategies (Component 1)

| Strategy | FairBanks Year 1 focus |
|---|---|
| 1 Surveillance / systems with governments | FCHIP capture -> MoH/NISS-aligned exports; data quality; simple GIS early warning |
| 2 Interoperable public health data | Community-facility-lab signal integration; weekly reporting; actionable dashboards for districts |
| 3 Public health programmes / service links | Community-facility links for HIV, TB, malaria, VHFs, mpox, immunisation campaigns |
| 4 Assessment and corrective action | Joint reviews with districts; AARs after drills; continuous improvement loop |
| 5 Workforce | CHW/VHT and facility training; surge roster; One Health-aware sessions |
| 6 Networks and surge connect | Partner coordination; MoH/CDC information sharing; surge activation pathways |
| 7 Small/large outbreak response | Components 2-5 contingency playbooks for moderate/substantial PHEs |

### B4. Anticipated outcomes

- Faster detection and response (7-1-7 style timing from community signal to district notify).  
- Stronger surveillance (weekly community reports; successful MoH-aligned export tests).  
- Ready workforce (trained CHWs/VHTs; surge roster exercised).  
- Better community-facility links (documented referral outcomes for priority flags).  
- Government use (tools, SOPs, and data access handed for MoH/CDC use).
