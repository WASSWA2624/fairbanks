## 1. Your organisation, country context, and the ongoing DPI rollout

FAIRBANKS MEDICAL CENTRE LIMITED is a Uganda private company limited by shares (Company No. 80020003843337, incorporated 4 October 2022). We run a UMDPC-licensed medical centre and pharmacy on Tirupati Road, Kyebando–Kisalosalo, Kawempe Division, Kampala.

Day to day we do more than clinic visits. Through FairBanks Community Reach we work with community members, Village Health Teams (VHTs) and community health workers, schools and local groups in Bukoto, Kyebando, Kisaasi, Kamwokya, Kikaaya and nearby areas. Care runs from home and community outreach into our medical centre. We also run GeriCare for older people, maternal and child support, chronic-disease screening, and a community health insurance scheme (CHIS) so families can share the cost of care.

The digital piece of this work is FCHIP — the FairBanks Community Health Intelligence Platform. It is already a working MVP. Frontline workers can capture household and outreach data on phones even when the network is weak. The platform brings community signals together with facility records. It also exposes secure data links so existing clinic EMR or hospital systems can send approved clinical data into FCHIP without forcing clinics to throw away the software they already use. Maps and simple risk flags help the team see where follow-up is needed.

In Uganda, digital tools for health are growing fast — identity for continuity of care, data exchange between community and facility, and dashboards that guide outreach. That is the DPI we are talking about here: sectoral digital health infrastructure that people feel in their homes and at the clinic desk. Our job in this Accelerator is not to sell software. It is to put practical safeguards into that live rollout while it is still young.

## 3. Track focus

**Issue-specific safeguards work.**

Over nine months we will put three concrete safeguards into the live FCHIP deployment:

1. **Consent and responsible data sharing** — clear consent language for household capture; purpose limits; least-privilege access for any EMR/facility data link; a simple audit trail of who accessed what.
2. **Offline and inclusion** — keep tools usable without steady internet; design for low literacy and local languages where we can; make sure women, older people, people with disabilities, and poorer urban households are not left out of the digital path.
3. **Grievance and recourse** — a named route for people to ask about their data, request a correction, or raise a concern about an alert or referral, with someone responsible and a response time.

We will start with a short risk map of the FCHIP data path (community → VHT → facility → dashboard) so the three measures sit in the right order. The map guides action; it is not the whole project.

## 4. Why this deployment moment matters for safeguards

FCHIP is past the blank-page stage. The MVP works. We are moving into real use with VHTs and our facility team. That is exactly when bad habits stick — weak consent, open data pipes, no way for a mother in Kyebando to challenge a wrong record.

If we wait until the platform is “finished,” fixing trust later will be harder and more expensive. Putting safeguards in now means consent, access rules, offline access, and a complaint path ship with the same workflows we are validating in Bukoto, Kyebando, Kisaasi, Kamwokya and Kikaaya.

Uganda’s digital health push needs examples that are grounded in a real clinic and real communities, not only national frameworks on paper. FairBanks can test safeguards where care actually happens, then share what worked.

## 5. Expected tangible outputs (6–9 months)

By the end of the nine-month cycle we will deliver and use:

1. A short **safeguards risk-and-gap note** for the FCHIP community–facility data path (who holds data, what can go wrong, what to fix first).
2. A **consent and data-sharing protocol** used in CHW/VHT capture and in any facility/EMR link (roles, purpose, access limits, audit).
3. An **inclusion checklist and field playbook** for offline use and for reaching underserved groups in our catchments.
4. A live **grievance / recourse pathway** with named owners, contact channel, and response times — tested with community members, not only written.
5. A small **working group** that meets monthly: FairBanks clinical and Community Reach leads, VHT representatives, a facility contact, and a KCCA/Kawempe Division liaison once confirmed — with simple meeting notes.
6. A **two-to-four page learning note** we can share with the DPI Safeguards Resource Hub on what a private community health facility learned embedding safeguards in sectoral digital health.

We will judge success by use in the field, not by documents sitting in a folder.

## 6. Coordination model (team, government, civil society)

**FairBanks Medical Centre.** Racheal Nabukeera (Managing Director) holds overall accountability. Clinical leadership and Community Reach coordinators run field tests. A technical lead wires consent prompts, access controls, and offline behaviour into FCHIP. A data-protection lead (named in Stage 2) owns the protocol and the grievance log.

**Government.** KCCA / Kawempe Division health focal point reviews priorities, joins the monthly working group when available, and is the path for the Stage 2 support letter. We stay aligned with how VHTs and private facilities already relate to Division health work.

**Civil society and affected users.** VHTs and community health workers from our catchments co-write consent wording people can understand. Community members — including women, older people, and GeriCare clients — help test offline tools and the complaint path. CHIS group representatives speak for families who pool money for care. Local community champions help monitor whether the grievance route is actually reachable. This is ongoing work, not a one-day workshop.

**Facility / systems side.** Pharmacy and clinic records staff advise on what data should never leave the facility without a clear reason. Any EMR link stays narrow and permission-based.

**Rhythm.** Kick-off workshop in month 1. Monthly working-group check-ins. Community listening sessions each quarter. End-of-cycle walkthrough showing the three safeguards running inside FCHIP and Community Reach.
