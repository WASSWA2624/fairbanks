## B. Stage 1 long answers (official form topics)

### 1. Your organisation, target country context, and the ongoing DPI rollout

FAIRBANKS MEDICAL CENTRE LIMITED (TIN 1053370026) is a Uganda private company limited by shares. Our principal place of business on the URA TIN certificate is Plot 1423 and 1425 Tirupati Road, Fairbanks Medical Centre, Kampala Central Division, Kololo IV, Lugogo, Kampala. Our public site lists the community location as Kyebando–Kisalosalo, Northern Bypass, Kampala (https://www.fairbanksmedicalcentre.org/). Slogan: Your health, our mission.

We run a UMDPC-licensed medical centre and pharmacy (URA activity: human health and social work). Services include general outpatient and inpatient care, 24/7 emergency care, diagnostics and laboratory, internal medicine, paediatrics, obstetrics and gynaecology, retail pharmacy, and vaccination programmes. Beyond the clinic we run FairBanks Community Reach and GeriCare, with Village Health Teams (VHTs), community health workers, schools, and local groups in Bukoto, Kyebando, Kisaasi, Kamwokya, Kikaaya and nearby Kampala communities. We also run maternal and child support, chronic-disease screening, and a community health insurance scheme (CHIS).

Ongoing DPI rollout: FCHIP — FairBanks Community Health Intelligence Platform (https://www.fairbanksmedicalcentre.org/fchip/). It is FairBanks’ Data & Feedback technology layer on Community Reach. A working MVP exists. The flow is Capture → Analyse → Alert → Act: offline mobile forms for CHWs/VHTs, secure links to facility systems (EMR/HMS), climate feeds, risk scoring and GIS, then worklists, facility dashboards, outreach, referrals, and follow-up. FCHIP connects CHWs/VHTs, schools and outreach, the Medical Centre and partner clinics, pharmacies where data exists, maternal/child, chronic disease and GeriCare programmes — with privacy and consent rules in mind. Clinics do not have to replace software they already use. This is sectoral digital health DPI. In this Accelerator we put practical safeguards into that live rollout — not sell a product.

### 3. Chosen track focus (System-Level Work or Issue-Specific Work)

Issue-Specific Work.

Over nine months we will advance three safeguards inside the live FCHIP deployment described on our public FCHIP page:

1. Consent and responsible data sharing — clear consent for household capture; purpose limits; least-privilege access for any facility/EMR data link; a simple audit trail. (FCHIP already states authenticated, consent-aware data APIs.)
2. Offline access and inclusion — offline-capable forms for CHWs/VHTs; design for low literacy and local language where we can; reach women, older people (including GeriCare clients), people with disabilities, and poorer urban households.
3. Grievance redress / user recourse — a named route to ask about data, request a correction, or raise a concern about an alert or referral, with an owner and a response time.

We will open with a short risk map of the FCHIP data path (community → VHT → facility → dashboard) so these three measures are sequenced correctly. The map guides the work; it is not the whole project.

### 4. Why this specific deployment moment is critical to advance safeguards

Our public FCHIP page states the stage clearly: a working MVP exists, and the focus now is validating it inside FairBanks’ live Community Reach — with CHWs/VHTs, facility teams, GIS and climate signals, and secure links to facility systems — before wider district and regional scale.

That is exactly when weak consent, loose data links, and no complaint path become hard to undo. If we wait until the platform feels “finished,” people in Kyebando–Kisalosalo and our other catchments may already distrust digital health tools — or be left out because tools only work online. Embedding consent, offline access, inclusion, and recourse now means safeguards travel with the same Capture → Analyse → Alert → Act workflows we are validating in Bukoto, Kyebando, Kisaasi, Kamwokya and Kikaaya.

Uganda needs safeguards examples from a real clinic and real communities. FairBanks can test that where care already happens — at the Medical Centre and in the homes we visit through Community Reach.

### 5. Expected tangible outputs from the 6–9 month Accelerator cycle

By month nine we will have delivered and used:

1. A short safeguards risk-and-gap note for the FCHIP community–facility data path (Capture → Analyse → Alert → Act).
2. A consent and data-sharing protocol used in VHT/community capture and any facility records link — aligned with the consent-aware API approach described on our FCHIP page.
3. An inclusion and offline-access checklist / field playbook for Kampala catchments, including Kyebando–Kisalosalo and neighbouring communities.
4. A live grievance / recourse pathway with named owners, contact channel (building on info@fairbanksmedicalcentre.org and facility phones), and response times — tested with community members.
5. A monthly working group (FairBanks clinical and Community Reach leads, VHT representatives, facility contact, and a KCCA Central Division liaison once confirmed) with simple meeting notes.
6. A two-to-four page learning note for the DPI Safeguards Resource Hub.

Success means these are used in daily work, not only filed away.

### 6. Coordination model — your team, the government counterpart, and local civil society / affected user groups

**FairBanks team.** Racheal Nabukeera (Managing Director and Co-founder) holds overall accountability. Clinical teams at the Medical Centre and Community Reach / GeriCare coordinators run field tests. A technical lead implements consent prompts, access controls, and offline behaviour in FCHIP. A data-protection lead (named in Stage 2) owns the protocol and the grievance log. Public contact for partners remains info@fairbanksmedicalcentre.org and +256 748 319 052 / +256 777 462 398.

**Government counterpart.** KCCA Central Division health focal point reviews priorities, joins the monthly working group when available, and is the path for the Stage 2 support letter.

**Civil society and affected users.** VHTs and community health workers co-write consent wording people can understand. Community members — including women, children (paediatrics programmes), older people and GeriCare clients — help test offline tools and the complaint path. CHIS group representatives speak for families who pool money for care. Local community champions in Kyebando–Kisalosalo and neighbouring catchments check whether the grievance route is reachable. This is ongoing work, not a single meeting.

**Facility side.** Clinic, pharmacy, and records staff advise on what data should not leave the facility without a clear reason. Any EMR link stays narrow and permission-based, matching our published approach: connect systems facilities already use.

**Rhythm.** Kick-off in month 1; monthly working-group check-ins; community listening each quarter; end-of-cycle walkthrough of the three safeguards in live use.
