## 3. UHPC presentation — Cooperative Health Insurance

**Presenter:** Georginah Byanjeru

### Who UHPC is

UHPC advances **cooperative health development** so communities can access quality, affordable care and better health outcomes. The vision shared: communities with reliable access to quality, affordable healthcare through health cooperatives.

### The problem

Many Ugandan households still face barriers to care:

- High out-of-pocket medical costs (about **41%** cited)
- Delayed care-seeking due to lack of money, leading to complications
- Selling assets or borrowing to pay medical bills
- Financial instability for health providers
- Poor outcomes, including preventable death, when treatment comes late

**Core question:** How can care stay affordable for families while providers remain financially viable?

### How the model works

Cooperative health insurance is a **risk-sharing / pooling** approach:

1. Members of an existing group (and often their households) pay a small **prepaid premium** to a preferred healthcare provider.
2. When someone needs care, the cost is shared across the pool—not carried by one family alone.
3. Members own and govern the scheme (rules, elected board, accountability for spending and performance).
4. The **healthcare facility** delivers clinical care, health education, and related services.
5. Members may also pay shares, membership, or subscription into the cooperative.
6. A **co-pay** at the point of care helps reduce misuse of services.
7. Providers may remit a share (about **10%** cited) into a cooperative reserve / solidarity fund.

### Benefits for members

- Timely access to quality, affordable care
- Predictable healthcare expenses through regular prepaid payments
- Financial protection (less need to sell assets for medical bills)
- Lower overall health spending through risk sharing
- Health education, prevention, earlier care-seeking, and better household wellbeing

### Benefits for providers

- More regular, predictable cash flow for salaries and medical supplies
- Stronger long-term relationships with clients
- Earlier treatment (members already prepaid)
- Better clinical and financial outcomes than unpaid emergency care

### Role of UHPC

UHPC builds capacity for health cooperatives and providers by:

- Supporting regulatory compliance and accountability to members
- Helping expand membership and member benefits
- Strengthening cooperative sustainability
- Training for quality, efficiency, and financial sustainability
- Promoting data-driven decisions, research, knowledge sharing, and collaboration

**Framing:** This is not only paying for treatment—it is investing in healthier families and stronger communities, and supporting Uganda’s path toward **universal health coverage**.

### Impact figures shared (to about 2024)

## 4. Questions and answers

### Q1 — Wasswa Wilson (FairBanks)

Impressed by the membership scale, female participation, and facility reach. Asked:

1. Are there **special packages** for different members, or one premium for all?
2. What services are covered or not covered?
3. How is the model **sustainable**, and how are services decided?

**UHPC response**

- Packages depend on the facility and the **group** that joins.
- Richer cover (for example chronic illness) can be arranged, usually with **higher premiums**.
- Within one joining group, members normally share **one similar package**.
- Covered services depend on what the partner facility can offer; facility and cooperative decide cover based on the agreed premium.
- Sustainability rests on member governance (board), facility participation, regular reviews, clear rules, and UHPC training and oversight—already shown across many facilities.

### Q2 — FairBanks (programme / community side)

Before a physical engagement, is there **existing literature** FairBanks can use to brief stakeholders (including VHTs / community health workers and local leaders), then invite UHPC for a consolidated meeting?

**UHPC response**

- Materials can be shared; more detail is available online.
- Public materials on cooperative health insurance and related project reports (partners including USAID were mentioned).
- UHPC can also share documents directly.

### Q3 — Diana Kawoozo (FairBanks)

Communities have circles, village savings groups, investment clubs, and formal cooperatives. **Which groups** can UHPC work with?

**UHPC response**

- Not every informal group is a legal **cooperative**. Registration with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Cooperatives matters for formal cooperative status.
- Savings and credit groups / SACCOs and similar member groups can still organise for health cover.
- What matters: a group that wants affordable care and can partner with a provider—agree premium, services, and an MoU.
- Workable settings named: community groups, SACCOs/savings groups, schools, employer groups, and similar organised groups.
- UHPC supports communities and **healthcare providers** to set up cooperative health insurance; it is not a conventional commercial insurer selling policies.

## 5. Partnership path and next steps

Wasswa stressed a real collaboration between **UHPC and FairBanks** for community benefit, and asked whether the next deep-dive should be physical or online.

**UHPC clarification**

- UHPC does **not** sell insurance as a conventional insurer.
- In partnership, **FairBanks Medical Centre** would be the facility offering cooperative health insurance to members.
- UHPC would **train** FairBanks staff and target group representatives—typically about **15–25 people** over about **three days** on roles, responsibilities, and how the model works.
- FairBanks organises participants; UHPC sends trainers.

**FairBanks response**

- The training path was accepted.
- Staff training should happen **before** a heavy community rollout so the team understands the programme in detail.
- FairBanks will place this on the **internal meeting agenda for 29 July 2026**, draft an **action plan**, and return to **Georginah and team** with a proposed **roadmap**.
